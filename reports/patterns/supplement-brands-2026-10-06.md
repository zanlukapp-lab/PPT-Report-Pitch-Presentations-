# Supplement, wellness and nutricosmetics brands - cross-brand patterns

- Run date: 2026-10-06. Analyst: brand-pattern-finder.
- Brands compared (5): MaryRuth's, Free Soul, Novomins, Vida Glow, SmartyPants. Sources: `reports/brands/<brand>.md` (all dated 2026-10-06).
- Category context: no Spate export in `inputs/category/`. Revuze not used.
- Memory: `memory/general.md`, `memory/patterns.md` and each brand file read. No brand-specific user facts exist, so no brand report needs a rerun for memory reasons.
- **Evidence caveat that applies to everything below.** All five brand reports were built from search-result snippets, because the network blocked direct page fetches (brand sites, Forbes, Lloyds, The Grocer, founder interviews and others). Exact figures, dates and wording have not been checked against full pages. Several success figures are also self-reported (Vida Glow units, Novomins velocity, Free Soul revenue) or third-party estimates (MaryRuth's revenue from Forbes, SmartyPants revenue from databases). For this reason no pattern below is rated higher than **medium**, even where all five brands support it.
- **Survivorship caveat.** All five brands are successes by their own measure. With no failed or stalled brands in the set, every shared trait below is a correlation, not a proven cause. See "Gaps" for brands to add.

## Summary

All five brands started with a founder's personal problem, proved demand online (own site and/or Amazon) before entering physical retail, and sell mainly to women buying for themselves or their families. Within that, the clearest shared lever is a product format or hero that makes the founding promise visible at a glance (liquid, gummy, sachet, mango greens), which then suits the brand's lead channel, whether that is TikTok, Amazon thumbnails or a pharmacy or beauty shelf. Three routes reach success: a marketplace-and-creator mass engine (MaryRuth's, Free Soul, earlier SmartyPants), a pharmacy-shelf need-state block (Novomins) and prestige-beauty placement backed by celebrity launches (Vida Glow), with the last two each resting on one brand only. The same warning signs appear in all five: range sprawl into trend SKUs and claims that run ahead of evidence, which has already produced lawsuits at SmartyPants and is the main EU/UK exposure for the others. Because the evidence is snippet-based and the set has no failures, these are medium-confidence correlations; timing (TikTok Shop, the gummy boom), category growth and founder audiences explain part of the result.

## Patterns

### Shared by most successful brands

**P1. A founder's personal problem is the origin story, and the story stays the same for years.**
- Description: every brand opens with a first-person problem (pill aversion, a family health scare, unfinished tubs, hair loss, a child who will not take vitamins). Story-clarity scores are 4-5 for all five, the highest-scoring criterion in the set.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's, Free Soul, Novomins, Vida Glow, SmartyPants (5).
- Exceptions: none in the set. But this is near-universal in the category, including in brands that fail, so it is probably necessary rather than distinctive.
- Confidence: **medium** (all five support it; survivorship makes it weak as a cause).

**P2. The format or hero makes the promise visible at a glance.**
- Description: the product itself is the proof of the story, which means it can be shown in a 15-second video, an Amazon thumbnail or a shelf facing without explanation. Liquid for "no pills" (MaryRuth's), gummy for "kids will take it" and "easy to remember" (SmartyPants, Novomins), a single 100%-collagen sachet for "the ingredient that fixed my hair" (Vida Glow).
- Supporting: MaryRuth's, SmartyPants, Novomins, Vida Glow (4).
- Exceptions: Free Soul differentiates by audience (women-first), not by format; its products are standard powders and tablets, and its viral hero (Mango Greens) is a flavour and positioning win within an existing format. Partial: Novomins has no single hero, it has a format applied to a 40+ SKU range.
- Confidence: **medium**.

**P3. Demand was proven online before physical retail, and retail then followed that proven demand.**
- Description: each brand built evidence of demand (reviews, sales velocity, social virality) online, then used it to win retail listings. The gap between launch and major retail was 2-7 years.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's (Amazon 2014 -> Target and Whole Foods 2021), Free Soul (DTC 2017 -> UK multiples 2024-25), Novomins (DTC/Amazon 2020 -> Chemist Warehouse IE 2022), SmartyPants (DTC/Amazon 2010 -> 30,000 doors by 2018) (4).
- Exceptions: Vida Glow moved into Australian health-food wholesale within two years of launch, so retail ran alongside DTC rather than after it. Its prestige retail push (2021-22) did follow years of DTC and China demand.
- Confidence: **medium**. Alternative explanation: this is the standard route for any small brand without retail funding, so it describes the category more than it explains success.

**P4. Amazon was an early growth engine, not a later add-on.**
- Description: the brands treated Amazon as a primary channel from the start, using reviews and search rank as both a sales channel and proof for retail buyers.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's ("top VMS brand on Amazon"), SmartyPants (Amazon "a particular focus", algorithm-led), Novomins (Amazon UK early, 5.0 seller rating) (3). Free Soul has a "strong Amazon channel" but its start date is not found.
- Exceptions: Vida Glow (Amazon presence unclear, may be a reseller). Free Soul is unconfirmed.
- Confidence: **medium**. Weakened by heavy Amazon discounting at MaryRuth's and SmartyPants, which means rank may partly be bought.

**P5. Social commerce and creators turn the founder story into sales at scale.**
- Description: the founder's voice is multiplied by creators who resemble the founder or the buyer (nurses and moms for MaryRuth's, moms for SmartyPants, women-to-women LIVE sessions for Free Soul), and in the two newest cases sales happen on the same platform (TikTok Shop).
- Supporting: MaryRuth's (TikTok Shop #4 seller, Jan 2025; 1,000+ creators), Free Soul (two Super Brand Days), SmartyPants (mom-influencer programme, 26M+ reach) (3).
- Exceptions: Novomins (no social or creator evidence found, a gap rather than evidence of absence). Vida Glow uses celebrities and founder Instagram rather than creator commerce.
- Confidence: **medium**. Timing explains part of this: TikTok Shop opened in the UK in 2021 and the US in late 2023, so MaryRuth's and Free Soul were early on a new channel with low competition and platform support. Most of the evidence is from platform and vendor case studies, which are self-promotional.

**P6. Women are the buyer, and ranges are organised by women's life stages and need-states.**
- Description: every brand sells mainly to women, for themselves (cycle, perimenopause, beauty, intimate health) or for their families (kids, prenatal). Need-state naming maps onto how retail fixtures are organised.
- Supporting: all five (5).
- Exceptions: none, though SmartyPants and MaryRuth's address "the mother as buyer for the family" rather than "women's health" as such.
- Confidence: **medium**. Women are the majority supplement buyer, so this is close to a category trait; it does not separate winners from losers on its own.

**P7. Beauty-from-within is a growth extension even for non-beauty brands.**
- Description: four brands either started in or moved into hair, skin and collagen, and in two cases that extension became a top seller.
- Supporting: Vida Glow (core), MaryRuth's (Hair Growth liquid, #2 TikTok Shop product Jan 2025), Novomins (Collagen gummies, a stated bestseller), Free Soul (CreaGlow creatine-collagen, Female Multivitamin with Collagen) (4).
- Exceptions: SmartyPants (no beauty line found).
- Confidence: **medium**. Relevant to nutricosmetics: beauty claims carry the highest claim risk (see W2).

**P8. A trust stack of third-party signals stands in for product-level clinical trials.**
- Description: none of the five has a published clinical trial on a finished product. Trust comes instead from certifications (B Corp, Clean Label Project, Vegan Society), contaminant testing, cause programmes, clinician founders or ingredient-supplier studies.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's (B Corp, Clean Label Project), Novomins (B Corp, Vegan Society, clinician founders), SmartyPants (Clean Label Project, third-party testing, Vitamin Angels) (3).
- Exceptions: Free Soul (Informed Sport page exists, SKUs unconfirmed; relies on customer surveys); Vida Glow (no certifications found; relies on brand-run, unpublished studies and celebrity).
- Confidence: **medium**. The "no finished-product clinical trial" half applies to all five; the certification half to three.

**P9. Founder-controlled, capital-light growth.**
- Description: four brands grew on founder money, an angel or debt rather than venture equity, and three still have founders in control.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's (bootstrapped to about $100M, took PE in 2021, bought it back with debt in 2024), Free Soul (self-funded, one angel round), Novomins (£70k savings plus Lloyds debt) (3).
- Exceptions: SmartyPants (crowdfunding, then PE, then sale to Unilever). Vida Glow (funding not found).
- Confidence: **medium**. Alternative explanation: profitable founders talk about bootstrapping more, and press repeats it, so the trait may be over-reported among winners.

### Different routes to the same result

**P10. Deep home market vs early internationalisation.**
- Description: three brands scaled almost entirely in one home market and left international sales unmanaged (grey imports); two expanded internationally early, each through a specific partner or demand pocket.
- Home-market depth: MaryRuth's (US), SmartyPants (US), Free Soul (UK, plus Ireland) (3).
- Early international: Novomins (Ireland, Australia, Germany through Chemist Warehouse and online pharmacy), Vida Glow (China demand, then US and UK prestige) (2).
- Confidence: **medium** for the home-market route; **early signal** for the international route (two brands).

**P11. Channel destination differs: mass/club, pharmacy, or prestige beauty.**
- Description: the online-first start is shared, but the retail destination matches price tier and story. Mass and club suits a family staple (MaryRuth's, SmartyPants, Free Soul's grocery listings); pharmacy and health-store fixtures suit a need-state gummy block (Novomins); prestige beauty suits a sachet sold as beauty at US$50 (Vida Glow).
- Supporting: see the models section.
- Confidence: **medium** for mass; **early signal** for pharmacy and for prestige (one brand each).

**P12. Success by exit vs success by founder-retained scale.**
- Description: SmartyPants succeeded by selling to a strategic buyer; MaryRuth's, Free Soul and Novomins by growing while keeping control. Vida Glow is unknown.
- Confidence: **early signal** for the exit route (one brand).

### Warning signs: where story and product or channel pulled apart

**W1. Range sprawl into trend SKUs that contradict the founding promise.**
- Description: every report flags SKUs that work against the core story: 300+ SKUs vs "make wellness simple" (MaryRuth's), Sculpt and "glow" vs "how women feel, not how they look" (Free Soul), NAD+, shilajit and libido vs "established science, not trends" (Novomins), acne probiotics, sleep, topicals and a tea collaboration vs "the marine collagen brand" (Vida Glow), sleep, fiber and pet SKUs vs "all-in-one" (SmartyPants).
- Supporting: all five (5). Story-product fit is the lowest or joint-lowest score for four of the five brands.
- What followed: no commercial damage is documented yet for any brand. At SmartyPants, sprawl coincides with a post-acquisition period in which the brand is not named among Unilever's growth leaders (weak signal).
- Confidence: **medium** that sprawl is common; **early signal** that it harms results, because no outcome data shows it.

**W2. Claims escalation beyond the evidence and beyond home-market rules.**
- Description: growth heroes increasingly rely on claims that the founder story did not promise and that the evidence base does not fully support: "clinically shown" hair growth based on an ingredient-supplier study plus 10,000 mcg biotin (MaryRuth's), customer-survey percentages and "hormone balance" (Free Soul), disease-adjacent naming such as "Endo" and low-dose collagen (Novomins), "clinically proven" and "world's No.1" without published data (Vida Glow), "Complete" and fiber claims (SmartyPants).
- Supporting: all five (5).
- What followed: SmartyPants faces two class actions aimed directly at its core promise (2021 "Complete", 2025 fiber). MaryRuth's had a 2021 infant probiotic recall (a safety rather than claims event) with no lasting damage found. For the other three, no ASA, FTC or court action was found; the risk is mainly that these claims block or reshape EU/UK entry.
- Confidence: **medium** that it is common; **early signal** for consequences (one brand with realised legal action).

**W3. "Premium" story vs deep discounting.**
- Description: list prices say premium while the price actually paid is often value-tier.
- Supporting: MaryRuth's (50% off list on Amazon), SmartyPants (120 ct for $5-9 with coupons), Novomins (H&B 15-20% multibuys) (3).
- Exceptions: Vida Glow (only 15% subscription discount found); Free Soul (25% first-order affiliate offers, not persistent deep discounting).
- What followed: no measured effect on margin found; MaryRuth's still reports about 20% EBITDA (Forbes estimate).
- Confidence: **medium** that it occurs; effect not shown.

**W4. "Global" story vs unmanaged international presence.**
- Description: brands that describe themselves as global or as category leaders have no official presence in large markets: MaryRuth's (grey-market EU/UK), Free Soul ("4 million women globally", no US), Novomins (US push announced c. 2022, by 2026 only a third-party Amazon listing), SmartyPants (no EU/UK under Unilever).
- Supporting: 4.
- Exceptions: Vida Glow (official presence in AU, US, UK; EU light).
- Confidence: **medium**.

## Alternative explanations

- **Timing.** TikTok Shop launched in the UK (2021) and US (late 2023) with heavy platform support for early brands; MaryRuth's and Free Soul were early and visible. SmartyPants rode the 2010-2020 rise of gummies and clean label. Vida Glow launched in 2014 as ingestible collagen was emerging, and caught Chinese cross-border demand in 2017. Each brand's main channel or format was growing fast when it entered.
- **Category growth.** Gummy supplements grew 7.9% in 2025 (New Hope, US data, cited in the Novomins report); collagen, greens and women's health have been fast-growing need-states. Without Spate category data, brand share gain cannot be separated from category growth for any of the five.
- **Funding.** MaryRuth's took PE in 2021 at the point it entered mass retail; SmartyPants raised repeatedly before 30,000 doors; Novomins used Lloyds debt for international retail. Capital access, not strategy alone, may explain the speed of retail rollout.
- **Founder audience and networks.** MaryRuth's started with the founder's coaching clients; Vida Glow used the founders' celebrity and fashion network (Rita Ora, later a Typebea co-founder); Free Soul used diaspora and business press. These are hard to copy and could explain early traction as well as any strategy.
- **Measurement.** The success evidence is uneven: Forbes estimates (MaryRuth's), company-stated and press-ranked figures (Free Soul, Novomins), unverified unit claims (Vida Glow) and an undisclosed exit price (SmartyPants). Some "successes" may be smaller than presented.

## Implications for a new brand or product launch

1. **Choose a format or hero that proves the promise by itself.** If the product needs a paragraph to explain why it is different, it will not travel on TikTok, Amazon or a retail shelf (P2). Free Soul shows the alternative is a sharp audience position, but it still needed a viral hero (Mango Greens) to break out.
2. **Plan the online-proof-then-retail sequence on purpose.** Collect reviews, velocity and repeat data in DTC and Amazon first, then take them to retail buyers (P3, P4). Design trial-size sachets or small packs for retail entry price points, as Free Soul and Vida Glow did (early signal).
3. **Match the retail destination to price and story.** Mass and club for a family staple, pharmacy for need-state blocks, prestige beauty for a beauty-priced ingestible (P11). Mixing them (Vida Glow at Harrods and at Nasty Gal) creates price and positioning tension.
4. **Build the trust stack early.** With no finished-product clinical trial, certifications, testing and clinician or educator voices did the reassuring for three brands (P8). For a nutricosmetics launch, a finished-product study would be a real differentiator, since none of the five has one.
5. **Write claims for the strictest target market from day one.** All five brands' growth claims would need rework under EFSA/GB rules (W2). A brand planning UK/EU sales should build heroes around authorised nutrient claims (e.g. biotin, zinc, vitamin C for skin, hair and collagen formation) rather than adapting US claims later.
6. **Set rules for line extension.** Every brand drifted into trend SKUs (W1). A simple test: does the new SKU make the founding promise more tangible, or does it borrow a trend? Beauty extensions worked commercially for four brands (P7) but carry the highest claim risk.
7. **Treat social commerce as a time-limited window.** Early entry to a new platform (TikTok Shop) gave outsized reach to two brands; that advantage shrinks as the platform fills. A new brand should look for the next under-served channel rather than assume the same result.

## Gaps

**Evidence quality**
- All five brand reports rest on search-result snippets because direct fetches were blocked. Rerunning brand-analyst for each brand with full page access (brand sites, Forbes, Lloyds, The Grocer, Sunday Times 100, Unilever releases) would let several patterns move from medium to high confidence. Priorities: Free Soul and Novomins revenue (Companies House, Sunday Times 100 entries), Vida Glow revenue and ownership (ASIC), SmartyPants post-2020 performance.
- Channel revenue mix (Amazon vs DTC vs retail vs TikTok Shop) was not found for any brand. This is the single biggest gap for P4 and P5.
- Novomins social and creator activity was not found; checking it would confirm or break P5.

**Failed or stalled brands to add (the set has none)**

These are candidates from general category knowledge, not from the brand reports; their status should be checked by a brand-analyst run before use:
- **Care/of** (US personalised vitamin packs, Bayer-owned; reported closed in 2024): DTC subscription model with no retail-first proof; a test of P3.
- **Sugarbear** (US influencer-led hair gummies): celebrity and influencer demand without a trust stack; a test of P5 and P8.
- **Goli Nutrition** (US ACV gummies, viral c. 2020, reported financial difficulties later): a format-led hero that rode a trend; a test of P2 and of the timing explanation.
- **SmartyPants after 2020** (already in the set): a possible stall under corporate ownership, which could be tested with Unilever segment data and Spate brand search.
- Comparable survivors that would widen the base for single-brand models: **Bears With Benefits** and **Vitabiotics** gummies (UK pharmacy model, alongside Novomins), **Absolute Collagen** or **The Beauty Chef** (prestige ingestible beauty, alongside Vida Glow), **Women's Best** (women-first, alongside Free Soul), **Olly** (US gummy mass, alongside SmartyPants).
