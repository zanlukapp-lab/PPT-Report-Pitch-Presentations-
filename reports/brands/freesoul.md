## Summary

Free Soul is a London-founded, family-owned women's nutrition brand (mother-and-son founders Rohini and Arjun Sofat, founded 2017 per most sources) that grew from gym-floor sampling into a reported £42m-sales business in the year to April 2026, ranking 10th in the Sunday Times 100 fastest-growing private companies. Its story, "female-first nutrition" for how women feel rather than how they look, is made tangible through protein, greens, creatine-collagen and life-stage products formulated with nutritionists and branded ingredients (KSM-66, Naticol, Actazin, Livaux). The commercial engine is DTC (Shopify, subscriptions) amplified by TikTok, where it is reported as the first UK brand to host two TikTok Shop Super Brand Days (Oct 2024, Jan 2026), followed by broad UK retail (Holland & Barrett, Boots, Tesco, Superdrug, Sainsbury's, Waitrose, Ocado). The most plausible success drivers are a clear women-only positioning in a male-coded category, a viral hero (Mango Greens, 17m+ servings), early and deep TikTok Shop commitment, and a retail rollout that turned social demand into mass-market reach. The main gaps are the US (no presence found), thin EU footprint, product sprawl, and heavy reliance on consumer-survey rather than clinical evidence.

## 1. Brand story

- **Origin.** Founded by Rohini Sofat and her son Arjun Sofat after Rohini's husband (Arjun's father) suffered a serious health scare; Rohini looked for supplements and found products "aimed at men" or women's products "focused on weight loss and appearance", not "how women actually felt". REPORTED (Eastern Eye, 2026; FITT Insider press release, c.2025-26). Inspiration credited to Arjun's grandmother, a trained nutritionist who championed natural ingredients such as ashwagandha. CONFIRMED (brand press release via FITT Insider).
- **Founding date is inconsistent across sources:** 2016 (Vegan Food & Living), 2017 (FITT Insider press release; febe G100 2026), 2018 (FoodBev / The Plant Base investment story; CB Insights). This report uses **2017** as the brand's own stated year. CONFIRMED (brand press release), with conflict noted.
- **Founders.** Arjun Sofat (CEO) previously worked in the City of London. REPORTED (Vegan Food & Living, date not found). Rohini Sofat, co-founder, described as the driving force behind the concept. REPORTED (Vegan Food & Living; Yours, date not found).
- **Bootstrapped origin myth.** No external funding at first; Arjun ran a gardening business and Rohini fitted kitchens and bathrooms to fund the first production run; Arjun delivered orders in a borrowed car. CONFIRMED (brand press release, FITT Insider). Early launch email produced "zero website visits"; the founders then drove to East London gyms at 4am to hand out samples. REPORTED (Shopify UK blog, date not found).
- **Core promise / positioning (brand's own words):** "Free Soul is a female-first nutrition brand building the Home of Women's Wellbeing." CONFIRMED (FITT Insider / TikTok Shop press release, Jan 2026). Also: "navigating women's health has been too difficult for too long." CONFIRMED (febe G100 2026 profile, quoting the brand).
- **Values and tone.** Natural ingredients, "free from artificial ingredients", nutritionist-formulated, education and community-led; tone is warm, inclusive and empowering, not aesthetic or diet-led. CONFIRMED (brand copy on retailer listings, Holland & Barrett, accessed 2026-10-06).
- **Target customer.** Women "at all ages and stages" (Health Club Management, first-year launch story), from gym-goers (protein, Sculpt) to peri-menopausal women (Peri-Menopause tablets) and wellness-led millennials (Greens, CreaGlow). The co-founder's own "I feel better at 50 than I did at 30" (Yours) widens the appeal to midlife women. INFERRED from range and press.

**Hero products and the story element each makes tangible**

- **Mango Greens** ("internet-famous", 17m+ servings sold, CONFIRMED by brand press release via FITT Insider). It makes *natural ingredients + grandmother's ashwagandha* tangible: KSM-66 ashwagandha is the link back to the origin story, and the mango flavour and "gut-friendly" framing make greens approachable for women who are not gym users. Evidence offered: a community survey (94% felt improved wellbeing after a month; 93% greater digestive ease). That is a customer-perception survey, not a clinical trial. CONFIRMED that the claim is made; INFERRED that it is non-clinical.
- **Vegan / whey protein blends** (the original product). They make *"supplements built for women, not men"* tangible: protein plus iron, folic acid, B6 and B12 tied to the menstrual cycle ("supporting you through every stage of your cycle"). Claims shown on Holland & Barrett use authorised GB/EU nutrient claim wording. CONFIRMED.
- **CreaGlow.** Makes *"how women feel, not just how they look"* only partly tangible: it bundles a strength ingredient (creatine) with beauty ingredients (Naticol collagen, HA, biotin), which is the brand's bridge into nutricosmetics. Naticol carries its own clinical dossier; HA has no authorised health claim in GB/EU, so the "radiant-looking skin" wording relies on biotin and vitamin C. INFERRED.

**Products that fit the story less well**

- **Sculpt "performance protein"**: the name and framing lean towards body-shaping, close to the "appearance" focus the founders say they reacted against. INFERRED.
- **Teas and early "Her PWR" tablets**: no longer visible in current retail; suggests early range experimentation. INFERRED.
- **Range breadth** (protein, meal, greens, creatine, collagen, multi, menopause, gummies, hydration) risks diluting the "home of women's wellbeing" into a catalogue of trends. INFERRED.

**Quality signals.** Informed Sport hosts a Free Soul brand page in its certified-supplement search (sport.wetestyoutrust.com), which suggests at least some SKUs are batch-tested; which SKUs are certified was not confirmed. REPORTED (listing exists), detail not found. "UK made", vegan options, gluten- and dairy-free: CONFIRMED on listings. Organic: only the KSM-66 component is labelled organic. NSF: not found.

**Regulatory read.** UK/EU listings stick to authorised nutrient-function claims (blood formation, tiredness and fatigue, hormonal activity via B6). Ingredients such as ashwagandha, collagen and HA carry no authorised claims, so the brand leans on survey percentages and "clinically researched ingredient" language. No ASA ruling against Free Soul was found (search, 2026-10-06). INFERRED risk: survey-based wellbeing and "hormone balance" claims in paid social are the most likely area for ASA/CAP scrutiny.

## 5. Coherence

**Where elements reinforce each other**
- *Story to channel:* a family, women-to-women founder story is native to TikTok and live shopping, where trust is built through faces and conversation. TikTok Shop then turns that trust into sales on the same platform. INFERRED, supported by two Super Brand Days.
- *Story to product:* "built for women" is backed on pack by cycle-linked micronutrients (iron, folate, B vitamins), which are authorised claims and easy to explain. CONFIRMED (listings).
- *Social to retail:* viral SKUs (Greens, CreaGlow) were rolled out as sachets in grocery and Boots, which lets retailers list proven demand at low risk. INFERRED.
- *Price to audience:* about £15 multivitamins and £30/month greens are accessible for mass UK women, which suits Tesco and Superdrug distribution. INFERRED.

**Where they pull apart**
- *"How women feel, not how they look" vs Sculpt / CreaGlow "glow":* newer products move back towards appearance cues. INFERRED.
- *Natural, nutritionist-led vs survey-based claims:* expert endorsement is generic (no named experts found) and efficacy evidence is mostly customer surveys. INFERRED.
- *"Home of women's wellbeing" vs range sprawl:* nine-plus lines in under a decade, some (teas, Her PWR) apparently dropped. INFERRED.
- *Global story vs UK-only footprint:* "4 million women globally" and "international expansion" are not matched by any visible US or EU retail. INFERRED.

Not tested (no Spate data; see gaps).
4. **Sequenced channel expansion: DTC to TikTok Shop to UK mass retail.** Evidence: DTC/subscription origin; retail across H&B, Boots, Tesco, Superdrug, Sainsbury's and Waitrose by 2026; Ocado "new" flags. 5. **Bootstrapped, founder-led capital discipline.** Evidence: no institutional VC found; a single angel round (2021); "independently owned" and EBITDA positive. Tag: REPORTED. *What would contradict it:* undisclosed debt or institutional funding. Not found (Companies House filings not checked).
